AI audit

Start by seeing how AI is already used

Most teams are already using AI, officially or not. Before changing anything, we find out what is really happening and what you would like to happen. Then we show you how to get the most from it.

  1. 1

    How you use AI today

    Which tools people use, for what, and how often, including the tools nobody formally approved.

  2. 2

    How you want to use it

    The tasks that eat time, the ideas people have, and what better would look like for your team.

  3. 3

    Where it helps most

    We rank the opportunities by how often the task repeats, how much time it takes, the risk, and the value. Some will be a quick fix. Some will be a not-yet.

  4. 4

    How to use it safely

    What information should never go into an AI tool, where a person must review the result, and who decides.

  5. 5

    How to stay unified

    Shared tools, shared habits and shared rules, so your team works the same way instead of five different ways.

What you get: a plain-English summary of where you stand, a short list of recommended first steps, and a clear view of what is worth doing now and what can wait. If something needs a custom build, we scope it with you.

Custom builds

When the job goes beyond what your tools can do

Sometimes a problem is bigger than the tools you have. When that happens, we build something custom. To keep it simple and safe, we use a ladder that describes how much freedom the AI is given. We start on the lowest rung that solves the problem, and we climb only when the problem needs it.

  1. 00

    Ordinary software

    No AI runs when people use it. It follows fixed rules, like a scheduler, a calculator or a checklist tool.

  2. 01

    One request

    The AI does one job, once. For example, it turns approved facts into a draft update. A person reviews it.

  3. 02

    Fixed steps

    Several AI steps in a set order, where each step uses the last one's result, such as notes, then a summary, then a draft.

  4. 03

    Sorting and routing

    The AI reads an incoming request and sends it to the right path. Anything unclear or sensitive goes straight to a person.

  5. 04

    Side by side

    A fixed set of separate AI jobs runs at the same time, and the results are combined into one report.

  6. 05

    Lead and helpers

    For each new case, one AI plans the work, helpers carry out the pieces, and the lead pulls it together.

  7. 06

    Draft, check, revise

    The AI writes a draft, a second pass checks it against clear criteria, and it revises until it passes or hits a limit.

  8. 07

    Autonomous agent

    Given a goal and a short list of allowed tools, the AI decides its next step based on what it finds. It has the most freedom and the most guardrails.

Higher is not better. A simple tool that always works beats a clever one that sometimes surprises you. Most problems are solved on the lower rungs.

A person stays in charge. On every rung, the AI drafts and recommends. Sending, publishing, buying and changing real records need a person's approval.

Everyone knows what is being built. Labeling each project by rung gives you and us the same picture of what it does, what it costs to run, and what could go wrong.
